🧠 How the AI Ecological Analysis Works

The system first extracts approved biodiversity observations from the database. Each species is assigned a trophic role such as producer, herbivore, predator, or decomposer.

Population observations are then used to calculate population changes and the Shannon biodiversity index. A machine-learning regression model estimates the statistical relationship between species populations and the biodiversity index.

The ecological rules then interpret population changes according to the species' trophic role. For example, a substantial decline in producers may reduce food availability for herbivores, while a substantial decline in predators may allow prey populations to increase.

Important: The AI results are ecological assessments based on the available observation data and predefined food web relationships. They should be treated as decision-support information rather than proof of direct ecological causation.
